# Output geometry: 1080x1920 with square pixels, on every path

**Status:** design, written 2026-08-08. No implementation yet. Measurement is done and recorded in
`docs/engine-notes.md` §7h; the guard `eval-clip-geometry.ts` is committed and currently RED.

**One sentence.** Every clip the product has ever delivered is tagged `SAR 1216:1215`, so its display aspect
is `76:135` instead of `9:16`, because a 9:16 slice of a 1080-tall frame is 607.5px wide and `scale`
compensates for the rounding by tagging the output rather than stretching it.

---

## 1. The defect, measured

```
target                    1080x1920   SAR 1:1        DAR 9:16    (0.562500)
today, everywhere         1080x1920   SAR 1216:1215  DAR 76:135  (0.562963)
```

| population | result |
|---|---|
| delivered clips, probed from R2 with no re-encode | **62 of 62 wrong**, a single distinct value |
| legacy centre crop (`cut.ts`), synthesised | wrong |
| reframe `center`, `single`, `split`, `stream`, synthesised | wrong, all four |

**Cause.** `cropWidthFor(1080)` is 608 - 607.5 rounded up to an even number - and the crop is scaled to
exactly 1080x1920. `scale` **preserves display aspect**; it does not stretch to fill. It therefore absorbs
608/1080 against 1080/1920 by writing `SAR = 1216:1215`. The split tiles reproduce the identical fraction:
`crop=1216` scaled to 1080x960.

**Magnitude: 0.08%, or 0.9px across the frame.** It cannot be seen. That is the argument for a guard rather
than an eye, not an argument that it does not matter - the tag is what every downstream platform reads, a
player honouring SAR renders the raster as 1080.9x1920, and a re-encoder normalising to square pixels
resamples the whole frame to do it.

**What this is not.** The owner reported a clip that looked "not 9:16". That complaint was a graphic insert
the crop had followed (§7g), not this. This defect was found while investigating that one and is invisible
on its own. Fixing it will not change what he saw.

---

## 2. The invariant

> Every rendered clip is exactly **1080x1920**, `SAR 1:1`, `DAR 9:16`, on every layout and on the legacy
> fallback.

`SAR 1:1` and an absent SAR tag are **not** the same thing and the guard reports them apart. An untagged
stream is read as square by most players and as unknown by some; the product should state what it means.

---

## 3. The rule

> `setsar=1` immediately after every `scale` that produces output pixels.

Verified before being proposed, not assumed:

```
crop=w=608:h=ih:x=656:y=0,scale=1080:1920,setsar=1   ->   1080x1920  SAR 1:1  DAR 9:16
```

**Why after each scale rather than once at the end.** The complex graphs composite tiles onto a base with
`overlay`. Setting SAR only on the final output would leave the overlay inputs with three different pixel
aspects, which is the exact condition the stream branch's existing comment says ffmpeg 8.x segfaulted on
during design. The tiles and the base must agree before they meet.

**`setsar` is already in this codebase, on the wrong lines.** The stream branch carries `setsar=1` after the
cam and content scales - and the base chain beneath them does not, so the stream path composites square-pixel
tiles onto a non-square base and ships the base's tag. The split tiles have none at all. This change finishes
a job that was started and left half-applied.

---

## 4. Where the change lands

| file | line today | change |
|---|---|---|
| `reframe/filtergraph.ts` | `baseChain` = `crop=...,scale=1080:1920` | append `,setsar=1` |
| `reframe/filtergraph.ts` | split `[top]` and `[bottom]` tiles, `scale=1080:960` | append `,setsar=1` |
| `reframe/filtergraph.ts` | stream `[cam]`, `[cont]` | already correct, unchanged |
| `processors/cut.ts` | `buildCropFilter()` legacy fallback | append `,setsar=1` |

`cropWidthFor` is **not** touched. See §7.

The subtitle snippet keeps its position after the scale chain: `crop,scale,setsar=1,ass=...`. `ass` draws on
the scaled raster and is indifferent to the tag, and moving it would change the burn.

---

## 5. What changes, and what must not

**Changes:**

- the SAR/DAR tag on every clip rendered from now on;
- what a player displays, by 0.08% horizontally - a squeeze, imperceptible, and in the direction of correct;
- **the encoded bytes.** This is the important one. `eval-camera-invariance.ts` level 3 compares full renders
  to `<id>.base1.mp4` by md5, and those baselines were produced before this change. They will all differ, and
  that is the change working, not a regression. The baselines must be regenerated by `corpus-baseline.ts`
  **after** this ships, and the fact recorded - otherwise the next person reads a red level 3 as a planner
  regression, which is precisely the misreading §7e already had to unpick once.

**Must not change:**

- any `x` value in any plan, or the plan JSON at all - this is the compiler, not the planner;
- clip durations, frame counts, cut points, subtitle timing or cue text;
- encoder settings;
- the stream tile geometry from `stream-geometry.ts`;
- `cropWidthFor` / `tileWidthFor`.

## 6. Tests

**Offline, in the suite.** An assertion over the compiled graph string: **every `scale=` is immediately
followed by `setsar=1`**, on all four layouts and on the legacy filter, driven from the real
`buildFiltergraph` and `buildCutArgs`. This is the test that catches a future `scale` added without one.

It must be **mutation-tested before it is trusted**: delete one `setsar=1` from the implementation and the
test must fail. A regex assertion that happens to pass because it matched something else is the exact shape
of the two fake tests this project has already shipped (`feedback_test_matches_default`).

**Eighteen existing assertions pin the graph text** across `reframe-filtergraph.test.ts` (14),
`cut-args.test.ts` (3) and `render-reframe.test.ts` (1). They are updated as part of the change, not
deleted - each one is a real pin on a real string, and updating them is how the change is reviewed.

**End to end.** `eval-clip-geometry.ts` must go green on the synthetic half immediately. The delivered-clip
half stays RED until clips are re-rendered, because it reads files that already exist in R2 - that is
correct behaviour and must be stated in its output rather than worked around by narrowing the query.

---

## 7. Alternatives, and why each is worse

- **Output 1081x1920.** Exactly 9:16 is unreachable at 1080 wide, and an odd dimension is illegal for
  yuv420p chroma subsampling.
- **Crop 606 instead of 608.** Even, and moves the error four times further in the same direction
  (606/1080 = 0.5611).
- **`-aspect 9:16` on the encoder.** Sets container display aspect while leaving the stream's SAR alone; it
  papers over the tag without making the pixels square and can disagree with the stream metadata.
- **Change `cropWidthFor` to produce an exact ratio.** There is no even integer with 608's ratio. It would
  also move every crop x in every plan, which is a planner change wearing a geometry change's clothes.

---

## 8. Out of scope

- The stream tiles' own sub-pixel stretch. `stream-geometry.ts` cover-crops the webcam and re-derives both
  tile heights with `evenRound` so they sum to 1920 exactly; that rounding leaves each tile's crop aspect up
  to one even pixel from its output aspect, which `setsar=1` turns into a genuine sub-percent stretch. It is
  the intended trade and it is bounded. Not touched here.
- Everything in §7f's and §7g's deferred lists: sub-floor faces, merge-blindness, the missed `scdet` cuts,
  the insert detector, active-speaker selection, the face-detector post-filter.
